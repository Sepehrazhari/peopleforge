The problem is rarely the tool. It is the gap around it.

Three failure modes we see repeatedly, in companies of every size.

The workaround tax

The tool covers most of a process, so the rest lives in a spreadsheet beside it. Now the same information exists twice and disagrees, and every question needs two places checked before anyone can answer it.

Paying to grow

Per-seat pricing bills you more precisely as you hire, which is exactly when budget is tightest. Multiple part-fitting tools multiply that, and none of the spend ever becomes an asset you own.

The person-shaped dependency

Someone clever wires it together in Airtable or Notion. It works, until they leave and nobody else understands the automations, the permissions, or why that column exists.

What makes this different

Three things, in the order they usually matter to the people making the decision.

01

It fits the process you already run

We start from how your company actually works — your stages, your approvals, your definitions — and build to that. If your promotion process has a calibration step that no product supports, it gets built. You stop maintaining the gap between the tool and reality, which is where most HR admin time actually goes.

02

You own it instead of renting it

One build, no per-employee licence. Hiring ten more people does not increase what the system costs you. And because there is no subscription holding it hostage, the software keeps working whether or not you keep paying anyone — including us.

Do the sum on your own numbers

Take what you pay per employee per month for HR software. Multiply by your headcount. Multiply by thirty-six. That is your three-year cost for something you will never own — and it rises every time you hire. Compare that with a one-time build. We would rather you ran that arithmetic yourself than took our word for it.

03

Your data stays in your own cloud

The system is deployed into your own cloud account. Employee records, salary history and review notes stay inside infrastructure you control, rather than a vendor's. For EU companies that removes most of the awkward part of a GDPR conversation: you decide where it is hosted and who can reach it, and there is no third-party processor to justify.

When this is the wrong choice

Headcount is not the test — small companies with unusual processes often benefit sooner than large ones with conventional ones. These are the real disqualifiers.

  • You need payroll. Payroll is regulated, jurisdiction-specific and changes constantly. Use a specialist provider and integrate with it. Be wary of anyone who offers to build you one.
  • An off-the-shelf tool genuinely fits. If your process is conventional and a product already covers it without workarounds, buy the product. Paying for custom to arrive at the same place is a waste.
  • You need something running next week. Signing up for a SaaS tool takes an afternoon. Building takes weeks. If the deadline is immediate, buy something now and revisit this later.
  • Your process is still changing weekly. A custom system encodes decisions. If you have not settled how hiring or reviews work yet, wait until it stops moving — otherwise you are paying to set today's guess in stone.
  • Nobody internally will own it. Any system needs someone whose job includes keeping it current. If there is no such person, an external product with a support line is the safer choice.

Questions people ask

What is a custom HR system?

A custom HR system is people management software built around one organisation's existing processes rather than sold as a standard product. Instead of adapting how you work to fit a vendor's model, the system encodes the workflows you already use for hiring, reviews, progression and people data. It is typically deployed into infrastructure the company controls, so the company owns both the software and the data.

How is this different from Personio, BambooHR or HiBob?

Those are per-seat products: you pay monthly for every employee, indefinitely, and the bill grows each time you hire. They are well built, but you adapt your process to their model and your data sits in their infrastructure. Studio is a one-time build shaped around your process, deployed in your own cloud account, with no per-employee licence. If you stop paying us, the system keeps running.

Why not just build it in Airtable or Notion?

Airtable and Notion are good at structured lists and documents, and plenty of companies get a long way with them. They tend to break down on process logic: permissions that differ by role, steps that must happen in a set order, calculations that have to be right, and anything that needs to run without someone remembering to trigger it. They also charge per seat, and the setup usually lives in one person's head until that person leaves.

How much does a custom HR system cost?

It is quoted per project after a discovery call, because the cost depends entirely on how much you need it to do. The useful comparison is your own current spend: take what you pay per employee per month, multiply by headcount, multiply by thirty-six months. That is your three-year cost for software you will never own.

Who owns the system and the data?

You do. The system is deployed into your own cloud account, so employee data stays inside infrastructure you control and never sits on a vendor's servers. For companies in the EU this simplifies GDPR questions considerably, because you determine where the data is hosted and who can reach it.

Is a custom HR system only worth it for large companies?

No — headcount is the wrong test. What matters is whether your process is unusual enough that off-the-shelf tools force you into workarounds, and whether you are already paying per seat across several tools that each only partly fit. Small companies with distinctive processes often benefit sooner than large ones with conventional ones.

Does it handle payroll?

No, and you should be wary of anyone who says otherwise. Payroll is regulated, jurisdiction-specific and changes constantly, so it belongs with a specialist provider. A custom system integrates with your payroll provider rather than replacing it.

What happens when we need changes later?

Because you own the system and its source, changes can be made by us, by another developer, or by your own team. Ongoing support and new features are available as an optional subscription, but nothing stops working if you decline it.

How long does it take?

It depends on scope, and you get a fixed timeline in writing before anything starts rather than an open-ended engagement. A first version covering one or two workflows is a smaller undertaking than a system spanning the whole employee lifecycle, and we would usually suggest starting with the former.

Tell us what does not fit.

The intake form takes about two minutes and asks what you use today, what breaks, and what you need it to handle. We read it and reply personally. If it is not a fit, we will tell you that too.
